Lily

Scientific name: Lilium spp.

Symbolism: Purity, peace and renewal.

Special features

  • Usually has six petal-like parts.
  • Prominent stamens and a central pistil.
  • Comes in white, pink, orange and yellow varieties.

Chenille-stem idea: Shape long, curved petals around a centre, then add a green stem and leaves.

Rose

Scientific name: Rosa spp.

Symbolism: Love, friendship, beauty and appreciation.

Special features

  • Many overlapping petals.
  • Often has a pleasant fragrance.
  • Rose stems commonly have prickles.

Chenille-stem idea: Coil red or pink stems into layered petals; add a green stem, leaves and a small central spiral.

Tulip

Scientific name: Tulipa spp.

Symbolism: New beginnings, hope and happiness.

Special features

  • Often has six petal-like parts.
  • Distinctive cup-shaped flower.
  • Grows from a bulb and is popular in spring gardens.

Chenille-stem idea: Curve stems into broad oval petals and join them at the base to make a cup shape.

Lavender

Scientific name: Lavandula spp.

Symbolism: Calmness, peace and relaxation.

Special features

  • Clusters of small purple or violet flowers.
  • Narrow, often aromatic leaves.
  • Widely grown for its fragrant oils.

Chenille-stem idea: Attach small purple loops or buds along the top of a long green stem, then add narrow leaves.

Sunflower

Scientific name: Helianthus annuus

Symbolism: Happiness, energy, optimism and growth.

Special features

  • Large flower head with yellow ray florets around a central disk.
  • Seeds are an important food source.
  • Young flower heads can track the sun's movement.

Chenille-stem idea: Arrange long yellow loops around a brown circular centre; add a sturdy green stalk and broad leaves.

Quick Chenille-Stem Craft Guide

Gather chenille stems in your chosen flower colours, green stems for leaves, scissors and a ruler. Ask an adult for help if any cutting tool is needed.

1. Shape

Make loops, curves or pointed petals according to the flower design.

2. Build the centre

Twist the petal ends together and add a small centre detail where needed.

3. Add the stem

Twist a green chenille stem securely around the flower base.

4. Finish

Shape leaves, adjust the petals and gently check that all parts are secure.

Why Flowers Matter

Flowers support biodiversity by helping plants reproduce and by providing food for pollinators such as bees and butterflies. Some flowers also provide seeds or fruits that support wildlife and people.
